# Evidence Rubric

Use this rubric when a recommendation depends on research quality.

## Evidence Types

- Fact: directly supported by a primary source such as official docs, standards, filings, source code, release notes, pricing pages, or policy text.
- Signal: indirect evidence such as search visibility, commit activity, issue volume, hiring, ecosystem momentum, partner mentions, or analyst commentary.
- Testimonial: a named person's or organization's experience. Treat as context, not proof.
- Adoption evidence: measurable usage, customer lists, downloads, stars, package dependents, benchmark participation, enterprise references, or public case studies.
- Risk: a downside, uncertainty, constraint, abuse case, compliance issue, cost exposure, migration hazard, or operational burden.
- Recommendation: an action that follows from evidence plus the decision context.

## Confidence Levels

- High: primary sources agree, facts are recent enough for the decision, and there is direct evidence for the claim.
- Medium: sources are credible but incomplete, indirect, or partly dated.
- Low: evidence is anecdotal, inferred, vendor-framed, stale, or missing an important counterpoint.

## Source Handling

- Prefer primary sources for claims that affect a decision.
- Use secondary sources to find leads, not as the final basis for high-confidence claims.
- Capture source date, publication date, or retrieval date for fast-moving topics.
- Mark claims as unverified when source access is unavailable.
- Do not hide uncertainty behind confident language.

## Research Checks

- Current facts: browse or search when prices, laws, product features, security posture, APIs, standards, people, rankings, or recommendations may have changed.
- Comparisons: evaluate alternatives on the same dimensions.
- Conflicts: show disagreement instead of averaging it away.
- Decision fit: connect every important claim back to product value, technical fit, cost, reversibility, risk, or speed.
